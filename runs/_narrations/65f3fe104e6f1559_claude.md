## MAPPO runs away with the warehouse — but the real surprise is who comes second

**What the robots had to learn.** Picture four robots in a tiny warehouse. Their
job: drive to a requested shelf, haul it to a delivery station, and return it to
an empty slot — and they only ever get a reward (a single point) for *completing*
that whole loop themselves. No points for being close, no shared credit for a
teammate's work. Until a robot stumbles through an entire fetch-deliver-return
cycle by trial and error, the world gives it nothing to learn from. That is what
makes this benchmark hard, and it is why the early going is so slow.

**The training story.** All four teams start out flailing — high randomness
("entropy"), lots of bumping into each other in the aisles. Watch how they
diverge:

- **MAPPO** is the standout. It scores its first reliable deliveries around 5
  million steps, then climbs steadily to a final **36.2 deliveries per episode**
  (peaking at 42.8). Crucially, its deliveries are spread *evenly* across the
  whole episode (12.6 early / 11.7 middle / 11.9 late) — this is a team running
  a smooth, continuous operation. Aisle contention collapses from 31% of moves
  to under 2%: the robots learned traffic rules nobody wrote down.
- **IA2C** is the quiet hero. With each robot learning entirely on its own — no
  shared "coach" — it still reaches **20.0**, clearly the second-best team. But
  its deliveries are front-loaded (9.4 early, fading to 4.7 late): it clears the
  easy nearby requests fast, then struggles to keep the pipeline full.
- **IPPO** stalls at **5.4**. Its phase pattern is the giveaway — 5.2 deliveries
  early, then almost nothing (0.18, then 0.01). It learns a quick opening move
  and then gets stuck.
- **MAA2C** finishes last at **4.0**, and its randomness never really drops
  (entropy stays high at 0.44) — it never fully commits to a strategy.

**The twist.** The two teams with a centralized "coach" critic (MAPPO, MAA2C)
finished first and last. And **independent IA2C beat both centralized IPPO and
MAA2C** — so on this sparse-reward task, central coordination is not a free win;
it helps PPO enormously and the simpler A2C barely at all.

**Bottom line.** MAPPO wins decisively and, just as important, learns to operate
*sustainably* across the full episode. One honest caveat: MAPPO trained for 60
million steps versus 20 million for the others, so it had a head start — but the
20M-vs-20M race between IA2C, IPPO and MAA2C is fair, and there the lone-wolf
learner came out on top.

*Every number here is measured from the runs' results.csv — no embellishment.*
