## Four algorithms, one warehouse: what the curves actually tell us

**The task.** RWARE is a cooperative multi-agent benchmark with a deliberately
brutal reward: each agent gets +1 only when *it* completes a full
fetch→deliver→return cycle, and nothing otherwise. With four agents on a 500-step
episode, there is no learning signal until an agent accidentally completes the
whole loop. This sparse, individual-credit structure is the whole point — it's
what separates algorithms that can do credit assignment from those that can't.

**The four contenders** (a 2×2: independent vs centralized critic, A2C vs PPO):
- **MAPPO** (centralized + PPO) — final **36.2** deliveries/episode
- **IA2C** (independent + A2C) — final **20.0**
- **IPPO** (independent + PPO) — final **5.4**
- **MAA2C** (centralized + A2C) — final **4.0**

(Note: MAPPO trained 3× longer — 60M steps vs 20M — so its absolute lead is partly
a budget advantage. The fair, equal-budget race is IA2C vs IPPO vs MAA2C.)

**What to look for in the Compare tab.**
1. **Team return** — the scoreboard. MAPPO climbs highest and keeps going; the
   others plateau.
2. **Policy entropy** — a measure of how "undecided" each robot still is. Three
   of the four settle around 0.2 (committed behavior). MAA2C stays high at 0.44 —
   it never fully made up its mind, which is why it finished last.
3. **Contention** — how often a robot's forward move is blocked by a teammate.
   Every team drives this down, learning unwritten traffic rules; MAPPO gets it
   lowest (under 2%).
4. **Delivery phase split** — the subtle, revealing one. It shows how deliveries
   distribute across early/middle/late thirds of an episode. MAPPO is flat
   (~12/12/12): a sustained operation. IPPO is almost all early (5.2 / 0.2 /
   0.01): it learns one quick move then stalls. This metric exposes *quality* of
   behavior that the raw score hides.

**The lesson worth remembering.** A central "coach" critic isn't automatically
better. It transformed PPO (MAPPO is far ahead of IPPO) but actually held A2C
back (MAA2C trails the independent IA2C). And the simplest method of all — robots
learning alone — beat both centralized competitors at equal training time. In
multi-agent RL, more machinery is not always more performance.

*Every figure above is measured from the runs' results.csv.*
